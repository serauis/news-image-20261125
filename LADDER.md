## Pairwise record (top 6, viewed in full)

Criteria 1–2 tie across all candidates (same date, same 40-story list,
same 7 regions, same timeline and date-check). Decisions were made on
criteria 3 (reads clearly) and 5 (craft), plus hero weight.

- **c01 beats c04 (ship vs Kyiv, 40/A):** the ship is the day's most
  consequential development (two-sea escalation, Zelenskiy's confirmation);
  the Kyiv rescue is the most emotional single scene but a consequence,
  not the top development — and its art is weaker (the rescuer reads as a
  black blob, the lamp beam is odd). Highest-LOW: the ship's low is higher
  — the Kyiv rescue still stands prominent as story #3 in the grid, while
  the Kyiv hero's low on consequentiality is worse.
- **c01 beats c07 (ship vs Riyadh, 40/A):** Riyadh is a repeat strike
  (second hit in a week, 11 wounded) — less consequential than the two-sea
  escalation; its art is the weakest of the four (the smoke reads as a
  brown smudge on the tower).
- **c01 beats c09 (ship vs 700,000, 40/A):** the number panel is striking
  and was fixed mid-ladder (its first render had dot rows overlapping the
  subtitle and spilling out of the panel), but a statistic is not a story:
  criterion 1 wants specific events of the day, and the ship names two.
  Close on craft; ship wins on criteria 1–2.
- **c01 beats c11 (ship A vs ship B):** layout A has the stronger
  front-page hierarchy (masthead → hero image beside its headline → grid);
  B's squat full-width band shrinks the ship and leaves the headline
  floating on plain paper.
- **c01 beats c13 (ship A vs ship C, narrow):** C's gold masthead pops at
  thumbnail scale, but the navy masthead reads as a newspaper, the date has
  higher contrast (gold on navy), and A's art-left/text-right hero anchors
  the eye better. A is the proven front-page format of this family.
